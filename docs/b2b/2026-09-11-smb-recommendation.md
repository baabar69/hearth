# Selling Hearth to small businesses (10 to 100 staff): recommendation

Written 2026-09-11 after three research passes (internal, market, feasibility, all in `research/`) and a red-team review. The direction is the founder's: companies of 10 to 100 people, with Keepers sold as coaching-style support, not psychology. This is a recommendation, not a record of decisions made.

## The answer

Yes: make selling to small businesses the main effort until 30 November 2026, and keep B2C running in the background at $0 (the content machine, one approval a week). Outbound selling is the only customer channel the founder can run without an ad budget, and in a company of 10 to 100 the owner decides and pays by card, with no procurement.

Three things are deliberately left out:
- **HR benefit contracts.** Large employers' 2027 plans were largely set by mid-2026. Costs rise 8 to 9% in 2027, and EAPs cost $1 to $3 per employee per month or come free.
- **"Affordable".** Hearth cannot beat free. The gap to sell into is that people never use what their employer already has.
- **Psychology.** Titles and licensure follow the employee's location.

## What to sell

Sell a ladder, starting with the owner:

| Step | What | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A wellbeing coach (Keeper) for the owner | $99 a month (the existing Deep plan: a Sit every week) | One person decides; owners carry everyone and rarely have anyone. It works even when there is no team deal. |
| 2 | Team seats for staff who opt in | $59 a month per seat (Hearthside service: a Sit every two weeks, messages in between, a Friday note), minimum 3 seats for 3 months ($531 up front) | Recurring money. The owner sees only how many people use it, never what is said. |
| 3 | A 60-minute team session, open to everyone, where nobody has to share | $450, or free with 5 or more seats | It is how staff meet a Keeper and decide to join. |
| Gift | The gift pass: prepaid coaching an owner gives staff for Diwali (8 November) or year-end | 5 passes x 3 months x $59 = $885 up front | Owners already buy staff gifts in this season; it gives them a dated reason to pay now. |

**Keeper pay, suggested:**
- A Keeper is paid the same per member whoever pays the bill: what they would earn for the equivalent consumer plan. That is $31.20 for a seat and $79.20 for an owner on Deep.
- Hearth keeps the difference, which funds sales and admin. On a seat, that is $27.80 before fees.
- Team sessions get a fixed fee per session.
- All of this is the founder's decision.

**Example:** a 30-person company takes a coach for the owner plus 5 seats.
- Revenue is $394 a month, and $1,182 arrives up front on a 3-month prepay.
- On the pay rule above, Hearth keeps about $159 a month before fees.
- Five companies like this come to about $2,000 a month in revenue. That is first money and proof, not yet a business.

## How to describe it: results first (one lawyer-approved description, used everywhere)

The founder asked, 2026-09-11, to sell on results ("improve performance", "wellbeing") because owners do not know the word Keeper. The answer: lead with the result and name the role in the owner's words, a **personal wellbeing coach** ("we call them Keepers", said once).

Softened wording does not lower the legal bar. An implied claim counts the same as a stated one: the FTC judges the net impression on the listener, and any objective claim needs a reasonable basis, including to businesses. Since 16 May 2024 the Telemarketing Sales Rule also bans material misrepresentation on calls to businesses. So every result is one of three kinds.

**1. Say now (true today):**
- "A personal wellbeing coach for everyone on your team."
- "The one-to-one coaching usually kept for executives, for everyone."
- "The same coach every time, every two weeks."
- "Coaching for work and life. Not therapy."
- "You see the team's wellbeing average, never who said what." (once the wellbeing check is running)

**2. Say with the source:** always name the research, which is about coaching in general and not about Hearth yet.
- "Research on workplace coaching finds it improves wellbeing and performance." The 2014 meta-analysis (Theeboom, Beersma and van Vianen, *Journal of Positive Psychology*) found positive effects on performance and skills (g = 0.60), wellbeing (0.46), coping (0.43), work attitudes (0.54) and goal-directed self-regulation (0.74).
- "A 2016 review of workplace coaching found positive effects on performance at work." The 2016 meta-analysis (Jones, Woods and Guillaume, *Journal of Occupational and Organizational Psychology*) found overall δ = 0.36 across 17 studies. It excluded peer coaching.
- **Condition:** the research only applies if Keepers actually coach. In every call with company staff, use the same shape: what matters, what's in the way, and one next step.

**3. Earn with your own numbers (from about January):**
- **Measure every company from day one** with the WHO-5 Well-Being Index, which the WHO makes free to use: five questions, scored 0 to 100. Add two work questions. Ask at the start and every quarter.
- **Report it only as a team average**, and only when five or more people answer.
- **After 12 weeks, results lines become claimable**, for example "wellbeing scores rose from X to Y in 12 weeks (n people)".
- **"Improves performance" about Hearth itself** waits until something about work is measured, such as how often worries outside work got in the way of it.

**Never:**
- treats, cures, fixes
- therapy, psychologist, counselling, mental health treatment
- the names of conditions
- reduces turnover by X%, guaranteed ROI
- licensed, certified coach (until a Keeper actually holds ICF certification)
- that Hearth is an EAP
- HIPAA-compliant, 100% confidential groups, crisis support
- affordable, cheaper

Prefer "wellbeing" to "mental wellbeing" or "mental health". The more it sounds like health care, the more an employer's lawyer treats it as a health plan, and the sharper the licensing question for coaches trained abroad.

**Keeper content rules:**
- No assessments, diagnoses, treatment plans or named clinical techniques.
- Anything clinical is referred out.
- The label "coaching" does not decide legality; what happens in the conversation does. Utah passed a 2025 law aimed at coaches practising therapy, and Colorado's coach exemption requires coach-specific training.
- Start the ICF coach-training path for the Keepers who will serve companies.

## Who to sell to

1. **Warm introductions (the main route):**
   - 20 owners this week, through the founder's, the Keepers' and family networks in the US and Canada.
   - Then South Asian chambers of commerce, business associations and owner networks in the GTA, Surrey, New Jersey, Houston, Dallas, Chicago and the Bay Area.
2. **Calls to owner-operators on public lines.**
   - **Sectors:** IT consulting and staffing, clinics, pharmacies and dental practices, home-care agencies, logistics and trucking, accounting and real-estate offices.
   - **Rules:** hand-dial only; no autodialer and no AI voice.
   - **Canada:** register with the National Do Not Call List (free) and call within the permitted hours.
   - **Hours:** US Eastern business hours are 18:00 to 02:00 in Pakistan until 1 November, then 19:00 to 03:00.
3. **Cold email to US owners.**
   - Send from a separate domain, never dearhearth.com, after 3 to 4 weeks of warm-up, so from about 5 to 12 October.
   - For Canada, use only addresses the person published themselves (CASL).

**What to expect:**
- **Calls:** about 1 meeting per 370 dials (Belkins, June 2026).
- **Email:** meetings from 0.8% to 1.5% of emails sent.
- **Warm introductions** convert far better, which is why they come first.

## Targets and the stop rule

**Targets:**
- 40 owner conversations.
- The first paying company by 10 October.
- 5 paying companies by 30 November.

**Stop rule:** if fewer than 3 companies are paying after 40 owner conversations, stop selling to companies as the main effort. Go back to B2C first (content, and Circles for individuals once they are live), and retry companies in spring 2027. Count conversations, not dials.

## Before the first cold call

1. **Deploy the site fixes.**
   - The Canada crisis line is now 9-8-8.
   - The insurance claim has been removed from /trust.
   - Both are ready and awaiting the founder's yes.
2. **Make every public claim true (a hard gate).**
   - **/trust** still claims things a buyer will read and a call must not repeat: AES-256 storage, AWS isolated databases, sign-in with 2FA, backups, a third-party audit, "HIPAA-comparable", "not readable on our servers", 120-hour training, fewer than 1 in 12 applicants accepted, monthly supervision.
   - **/pricing** includes Circles that do not run yet.
3. **A short fixed-fee legal review of the call script and one-pager.**
   - It covers the coaching wording, state counselling and social-work practice laws, and Ontario's controlled act of psychotherapy.
   - Limit it to Ontario and one or two US states to start.
   - Warm conversations can begin in parallel using only the "say" list.
4. **Name 2 to 3 real Keepers who consent.** Each gets a short bio: training, languages, background check, no protected titles. They must be available for 21:00 to midnight PKT sessions.
5. **Write the "Hearth for Teams" one-pager in plain business language.** The consumer site keeps its voice.
6. **Get an insurance quote from a broker now.** Cover must include US and Canada territory. Bind it from the first prepayment, before the first session.
7. **Get paid through a Whop invoice** (card or ACH), with a W-8BEN ready.
8. **Review the contract when the first buyer says yes.** Use the Common Paper services agreement and DPA plus a Hearth schedule (scope, not therapy, crisis protocol, aggregate-only reporting).

**Cost:**
- About $4,000 to $17,000 in the first year, mostly legal review and insurance, plus $70 to $130 a month in tools.
- The order above delays the contract review and insurance until a buyer has paid.

## Decisions only the founder can make

1. Keeper pay for company seats and sessions.
2. Whether to fix the remaining /trust and /pricing claims now (recommended before any call).
3. The budget for the legal review and insurance.
4. Which 2 to 3 real Keepers serve companies, and their consent to be named.
5. Commit and deploy the crisis-line fix and the insurance removal.
