# 30-day plan: break into the therapy and mental-health search space

Written 2026-08-22. Tracked as **KAN-48** under the topical authority epic (KAN-37).

Founder decision: attack the therapy and mental-health space directly rather than
staying in adjacent long tail. This is the route that actually gets us in.

## What 30 days will and will not do

Stated up front so nobody is surprised on day 31.

**Will not**: rank for `therapy`, `online therapy`, `mental health` or `betterhelp`.
Those head terms belong to BetterHelp, Talkspace, Psychology Today, NIMH, Healthline
and Psych Central. They are a 12 to 24 month outcome of doing this work repeatedly,
not a 30 day one. Anyone who says otherwise is selling something.

**Will**: put Hearth on the board inside that space through its long tail. These are
still therapy and mental-health queries. They are simply the specific ones, and
specificity is where a young domain can win. Getting several of them ranking is also
the mechanism by which head terms eventually become reachable, because topical
authority accumulates from the tail upward. There is no route to `therapy` that does
not pass through here first.

## Why these targets

Competitive research, 2026-08-22.

**`do I need therapy or just someone to talk to`** is currently topped by
**drrandifredricks.com, a single therapist's personal blog**, alongside Helpguide,
MHA Screening and NIMH. A solo practitioner outranking institutions means the query
is under-served and a better page can take it.

It is also almost a restatement of Hearth's proposition. Even the pages ranking today
conclude that not everyone needs therapy and that peer support is a legitimate answer.
We can write the best page on the internet for that question, because it is the
question the company was founded on.

**`can't afford therapy`** returns mentalhealthwellnessmhw.com, televerohealth.com,
helloklarity.com, rula.com and Psych Central. Small clinic blogs and health startups.
Psych Central is the only heavyweight in the set, and the rest are thin listicles that
largely repeat each other.

Neither SERP is Healthline and Mayo. That is the whole reason these two go first.

Both target people who have already chosen therapy, which removes any conflict in

### Week 3: distribution (KAN-53)

Four pages will exist. Publishing is not the same as being found. This week runs
KAN-39 (the roundup pitches, highest return item we have), KAN-24 (Trustpilot),
KAN-25 (F6S), KAN-26 (Medium and Substack), KAN-27 (LinkedIn article) and KAN-28
(Reddit and Quora).

Sequencing that matters: post the Reddit and Quora answers **after** the pages are
live, so there is something specific to link to. An answer linking to a real page that
addresses the exact question survives moderation. One linking to a homepage reads as
promotion and gets removed.

Expect nothing measurable this week.

### Week 4: measure honestly (KAN-54)

Baselines to measure against, all recorded 2026-08-21/22:

| Metric | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Queries containing "therapy" or "mental health" | **0** |
| Total impressions, 3 months | 203 |
| Total clicks, 3 months | 3 |
| Average position | 31.4 |
| Indexed pages | 37 |
| Hearth-keeper queries | 15 impressions, 0 clicks |

**Primary success measure**: impressions on at least 5 queries containing "therapy" or
"mental health". Today that is zero, so anything above zero proves the domain is being
reclassified away from "hearth, the fireplace".

**Judge the execution at day 30, not the strategy.** Were five pages published? Were
they good? Was distribution done? Those are answerable in 30 days. Whether the
strategy works is not: content typically takes 3 to 6 months to reach a stable
position, and longer in YMYL categories where Google is deliberately conservative.

## The rule that makes health content rank

Every page says plainly when therapy is the right answer, and carries crisis resources
near the top.

This is not caution slowing the work down. On health queries Google's raters reward
content that routes people to appropriate care rather than capturing them, and readers
detect the difference immediately. It is also the thing our competitors structurally
cannot do: a page written by a therapy platform cannot say "maybe therapy is not what
you need right now" without undermining its own product. We can, because our position
does not depend on it.

The same applies to `can't afford therapy`. Recommending 7 Cups, NAMI and warmlines
where they genuinely fit is what earns the right to be on that page at all.

## Blocker

**KAN-45**: whether the Keeper profiles are real people must be answered before
publishing more health-adjacent content. Google holds YMYL sites to a stricter
standard, the Keeper pages are currently our best-performing content, and building
more on an unresolved foundation risks the lot.

## Month 2 and beyond

Month 2 depends on what week 4 shows. If a query family produces clicks, write three
more pages in that family and follow the evidence rather than this plan.

The honest horizon: 6 to 12 months of this before topical authority shifts meaningfully,
and the head terms are a 12 to 24 month question that may never fully resolve given the
competition. What is achievable, and worth doing, is owning the specific queries where
Hearth is genuinely the best answer available.
